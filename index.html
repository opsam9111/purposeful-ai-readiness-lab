Step 1

Understand the adoption problem

Bringing a tool into a school is not the same as meaningful use. UTAUT helps us ask what may shape teachers' intention and actual use before leaders invest in technology.

1

Acceptance

What teachers believe about the tool.

2

Intention

Whether they plan to use it.

3

Implementation

How the school supports practice.

4

Continuance

Whether useful practice lasts.

↗

Performance expectancy

Will this help me prepare or teach more effectively?

Evidence to look for: better examples, useful feedback, or saved preparation time.
◎

Effort expectancy

Will this be understandable and manageable to learn?

Evidence to look for: simple steps, guided practice, and confidence after trying.
◌

Social influence

Do trusted colleagues and leaders support appropriate use?

Evidence to look for: peer modelling, leadership permission, and shared norms.
⌂

Facilitating conditions

Are devices, electricity, connectivity, policy, training, and help available?

Evidence to look for: realistic access and a named person who can help.
How the model works—and where it stops

UTAUT proposes that performance expectancy, effort expectancy, and social influence shape behavioral intention, while facilitating conditions and intention help explain use. Context can change the strength of these relationships.

UTAUT can diagnose acceptance conditions. It cannot decide whether AI output is accurate, whether a lesson is pedagogically sound, or whether a school policy is ethical. TPACK, UDL, professional judgment, and local policy address those questions.

Diagnose your school's starting point

Choose the response that best reflects your present situation. Honest answers produce a more useful plan.

Step 3

Turn the diagnosis into support

A low score is not a teacher failure. It points to a condition the school can improve.

PE

Make value visible

Pilot one authentic task and compare the result, time, and quality with the current method.

EE

Reduce avoidable effort

Use a short prompt template, guided practice, and a worked example before independent use.

SI

Build trusted support

Let a respected teacher model one safe use and invite questions without pressure.

FC

Strengthen conditions

Check devices, data, electricity, policy, training, and nominate a School GenAI Coordinator.

A feasible four-stage school roadmap

  1. Listen: collect teacher concerns and access realities.
  2. Try: run a small, voluntary lesson-planning pilot.
  3. Verify: review accuracy, bias, privacy, workload, and student benefit.
  4. Scale: share evidence, revise policy, and expand support only when justified.

Step 4

Demonstrate purposeful use: boiling-point investigation

This idealized model lets teachers explore how the amount and type of a nonvolatile dissolved solute may change water's predicted boiling point at 1 atm.

ΔTb = iKbm100.70 °CIdealized prediction; actual classroom measurements may differ.

Teacher prompt template

“Create three age-appropriate explanations of boiling-point elevation for beginning science students: one short text, one labelled diagram description, and one oral explanation script. State that the solute is nonvolatile, pressure is 1 atm, and the model is idealized. Do not invent experimental results.”

TPACK

Content sets the scientific boundaries; pedagogy uses prediction, investigation, and explanation; technology makes variable changes visible. The tool is selected because these three forms of knowledge work together.

UDL

Learners can read, listen, inspect a numerical model, and explain in writing, speech, or a labelled sketch. The learning target and success criteria remain consistent.

UTAUT

Teachers evaluate whether the activity is useful and manageable, while leaders examine social support and enabling conditions before wider adoption.

Responsible AI verification checkpoint
  • Check every scientific claim against a trusted chemistry source.
  • Remove names or identifiable student information before using an AI tool.
  • Review examples for cultural relevance, bias, reading level, and accessibility.
  • Disclose meaningful AI assistance and keep the teacher responsible for final decisions.

Limitations and accessibility notes

The readiness check is educational and should not be used to rank teachers. The boiling-point calculation is simplified. Browser voices differ, so written text is always provided.

The site supports keyboard navigation, visible focus, responsive layout, strong colour contrast, text alternatives, reduced motion, print, and browser read-aloud. The resource does not claim complete conformance until tested with representative users and assistive technologies.

Design claims are informed by UTAUT (Venkatesh et al., 2003), adoption research (Straub, 2009), Nigerian GenAI evidence (Adigun et al., 2025), TPACK (Mishra & Koehler, 2006), and UDL (CAST, 2024).
